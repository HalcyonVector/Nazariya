Wire desk · Near-duplicate detection

Own reporting, or agency copy?

Share of each outlet's articles that are its own, not credited PTI, ANI or IANS copy or text matching another outlet's earlier story.

Counted over articles whose text could be read. 95% Wilson intervals on hover.

Shelf life · Survival analysis

How long a story stays in the news

Share of stories still drawing new articles after a given time. A story ends after 72 hours with nothing new; stories still running count as unfinished.

Copy desk

Who copies whom

Text that near-duplicates an earlier article from a different outlet, with no agency credit.

Corrections and clarifications

Headlines changed after publishing

Feeds are read every four hours. When a story comes back under a new headline, both versions are kept. Spacing, case and quote marks are ignored.

Stories that disappeared

A sample of articles is checked again a day or two after publishing. One counts as removed only when it is clearly gone (not found, or sent to the front page) on two checks hours apart; blocked or failed checks never count.

Lost in translation

Where English and Hindi told different stories

For stories both languages covered, how far apart the English and Hindi articles sit in a multilingual embedding space.

Distance: 1 minus the cosine of the mean BGE-M3 vectors per language. Events with 2+ outlets in each language.

Breakout watch · forecast

Which of today's stories will spread

From a story's first two hours (how many outlets and languages have it, and how it broke), a model estimates the chance it reaches ten or more newsrooms. Its record on stories it had not seen is published alongside.

Who gets named · same stories, binomial tests

Who each newsroom puts in its headlines

For the stories an outlet covered, how often its headlines name each of the most-named people and parties, against the other outlets' headlines on the same stories. Pink: more than its peers; green: fewer. An outlined cell is a clear difference after correcting for the number of comparisons; the rest may be chance.

Language bridges

How stories cross languages

For stories three or more outlets covered: how often a story that broke in one language was carried in another, how long that took, and which outlets carried it across first.

Agenda fingerprints

What each newsroom spends its coverage on

Each outlet's share of stories in each frame, divided by the share across all outlets. Above 1x: it gives that kind of story more of its coverage than the field does.

Who owns the news · permutation test

Do outlets with the same owner cover the same stories?

For each pair of sister outlets, how much of their coverage overlaps, against pairs of different-owner outlets in the same languages. Ownership is taken from each outlet's Wikipedia article, cited on its file.

Official business · PIB pickup

Government announcements nobody ran

Every press release from the Press Information Bureau, matched to the news events it landed in. A release counts as reported if any tracked outlet covered that event within two days.

Ministry read from each release's opening line. 95% Wilson intervals on hover.

Each row is an outlet, each column an hour of the day in IST; darker means more of its stories first appeared then. Sorted by how much it publishes between 10 pm and 6 am.

About

Nazariya models how India's news outlets cover the same events. It measures coverage patterns, not truth or intent, and every estimate carries uncertainty. Source code

Data
  • Outlet RSS feeds and news sitemaps: headlines, times, links (list and status)
  • PIB: government press releases
  • Wikidata: people, parties and states named in articles
  • Census of India 2011: state populations
  • Wikipedia: who owns each outlet (cited on each outlet's file)
